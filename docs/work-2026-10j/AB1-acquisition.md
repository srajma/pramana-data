## Summary of typical ranges

- **Manuscript copies from Indian repositories:** about ₹10–25 per folio image where a rate is published (Adyar ₹10 per exposure taken by the reader; IGNCA/NMM ₹25 per page; NAI benchmark ₹20 Indian / ₹40 foreign per image). Most ORIs (BORI, Mysore, RORI, Kerala, Sarasvati Bhavan, Asiatic Society, Saraswathi Mahal) publish no rate. For them, the real costs are a permission letter, membership (₹250–500), a visit in person, and the custodian's consent.
- **Access, not price, is the main constraint:** NMM/Gyan Bharatam images are view-only online and released "in accordance with the rights of custodians" (in 2018 only 6 pages per manuscript were viewable). IGNCA copies need a signed use-statement plus the owner's MOU terms. Reader photography ranges from free (GOML, as of 2010–12) to ₹10 per shot (Adyar) to not allowed.
- **UK comparators:** new photography costs £5.50–£27 per image (Cambridge £25→£5.50 by volume, minimum £25 + VAT; Bodleian £27 then £7.50, minimum £20) with 20–30 day turnaround. Readers' own photography is free at the Bodleian, the BL (item by item for Asian and African manuscripts) and the Wellcome.
- **Doing it ourselves in India:** commercial book scanning costs ₹3–10 per page. A CZUR-class overhead scanner costs about ₹40–70k, one-off. The national RFP assumes 400–600 dpi face-up capture at a bid rate per page, and the whole Gyan Bharatam budget works out to about ₹1 per page for 50 crore pages. Archival-photographer day rates: unconfirmed.
- **Printed editions that are not online:** in-print reprints cost ₹55–1,000 (Chaukhambha) or ₹225–4,800 (Motilal). Out-of-print 19th/early-20th-century editions cost about US$100–250 used, plus about ₹1–4k to scan. Copyright, not cost, is the binding limit: free if the author/editor died more than 60 years ago (died before 1966), or if an anonymous or government edition was published before 1966. Otherwise the publisher's or heirs' permission is needed.
