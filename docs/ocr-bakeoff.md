Edition differences cost every engine the same, so the error rates are a ceiling — read them relative to each other.

**Excess error** is an engine's character error rate minus the best engine's on the same page — it removes the floor set by edition differences (e.g. ~23% on every Gopatha page). **Gross failures**: pages more than 20 points worse than the best engine, split into omissions (output < 75% of the passage) and additions (> 130%: repeated lines, hallucinated or duplicated text).

## Findings (run of 2026-10-06)

* **Claude Opus 5.5** was the best or tied-best engine on almost every page, but costs ~$56 per 1,000 pages.
* **Gemini 3.1 Pro** is within about a point of it at a quarter of the price (~$15 / 1,000 pages): the best
  value for texts that matter.
* **Gemini 3.8 Flash** (~$4.70) and **Datalab** (Chandra; balanced ~$3, accurate ~$8) are about 3 points
  behind the best — far better than Tesseract (22 points behind, mangling about half the words). Datalab
  also drops running heads and keeps layout; Gemini Flash returned one empty page (caught and retried by
  the engine layer now).
* **Claude Sonnet 5.5** declined or stopped early on 3 of 50 pages ("too dense to transcribe reliably");
  **GPT-5.2**, **Mistral** and the open **Qwen3-VL-32B** misread too much for this material.
* Batch APIs halve the Gemini prices. Test material: modern typeset prints (with apparatus and some
  commentary); older lithographs and manuscripts are harder and need their own test.
