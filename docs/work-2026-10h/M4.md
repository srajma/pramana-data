# M4 — Manuscript OCR bake-off: models × scripts × materials (round 2026-10h)

*Workstream M4 (sub-task of #111), 2026-10-23/24. Machine: 2 CPU / 4 GB. 69 manuscript pages, 14 vision models via
OpenRouter, plus three variants (wide leaves cut into pieces, high reasoning effort, cheap+strong cascades).
Experiment spend **≈ $27 ($25.13 recorded per call + ≈ $1.5 for calls whose records were discarded: shake-down loops, six high-effort calls stopped mid-way)** (per-call ledger in `raw_data/m4/out/**/*.json`). Nothing uploaded or deployed.
Full generated tables: [M4-tables.md](M4-tables.md). Script: `scripts/m4/bakeoff.py`. Test set: `raw_data/m4/`.*

## The user's question, answered

> "Why are manuscript scans more costly to OCR? Do we need more expensive models for it? Do we have any data on
> this in the wiki and if not can you do that?" (2026-10-24)

There was no data in the wiki: the earlier bake-offs (memories/model-choices) used printed books only, and the
estimate of ≈ $0.01–0.03 a page for manuscripts was a guess. This bake-off measures it.

1. **A manuscript page doesn't cost more per page. It costs 4–5× more per letter of text.** Models charge per
   image plus per output token. A folio carries little text: the median page here has **708 letters** (quartiles
   514–1,265), against **≈ 2,400** on a printed Sanskrit page. So a work needs **about 3.4× as many pages**.
2. **Yes, apart from clean Devanāgarī, it needs the stronger model.** Gemini 3.8 Flash, the bulk OCR model for
   printed books, is 3–4 points behind the best on prints and about 2 behind on clean Devanāgarī hands. Over all
   manuscripts it is **16 points** behind. **Gemini 4 Argon** is the best or near-best
   engine on almost every script, at **$0.008 a page**, 2.7× Flash. Argon instead of Flash, times 3.4× the pages,
   comes to **≈ $0.010 per 1,000 letters**, against ≈ $0.002 for printed books.
3. **Expected cost:** ≈ **$0.008 a page** with Argon. Per work: a Bhagavadgītā manuscript (~75 pages) ≈ **$0.60**; a
   400-page manuscript ≈ **$3.20**; a Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa (~2,500 pages) ≈ **$20**. The guess was about right per
   page and too high per work: $5–30 is reached only by the longest works, or by reading every page twice.
4. **For several scripts the binding constraint is not cost.** Every model misreads them: Śāradā, Nepalese palm
   leaves, Grantha, Odia, and partly Malayalam and Tamil palm leaves. Even the best has a character error rate of
   **38–80 %**, or refuses. Paying more doesn't help: Opus 5.5 costs 3× Argon and is worse there, and high
   reasoning effort costs 4–20× and gains at most 8 points. Only training on the script would.
5. **A cheap model with a strong one on the doubtful lines does not pay on manuscripts.** Flash and Flash Lite
   disagree on **90 % of the lines**, so almost everything is re-read. The cascade then costs **$0.012–0.015 a page**,
   which is more than Argon alone ($0.008), and is less accurate (33–40 % vs 27 %). On Devanāgarī alone, a
   page-level cascade reaches Argon's accuracy only at Argon's price.

**References aligned to the page.** For each page the e-text passage is cut to the stretch the folio carries:
the span of the reading that best matches it (`bakeoff.py align`). For pages that a reference does not follow line
by line (tripāṭha layouts, marginal commentary), transcription lines are matched anywhere in the passage and
joined in page order. For one Śāradā leaf, which no model could locate, the span was cut by hand after reading the
leaf (AHS Sū 1.8b–1.16). The Bengali palm leaf has no e-text, so its reference is Gemini 4 Argon's reading
corrected by eye; Argon is not scored on that page. On 12 pages no engine could be located in the passage at all.
There every engine gets only a *lower bound*: its best window anywhere in the passage. These are marked ≥ below.
Two Odia leaves without any text are not scored.

## 2. Method

* **Prompt** (identical for all; `PROMPT` in the script): transcribe line by line in IAST (ISO 15919 for Tamil or
  Malayalam text), exactly as written, no correction or completion from memory, `[…]` for illegible letters,
  daṇḍas as `|`, and folio numbers, marginalia and catchwords on lines starting `M:`. Temperature 0, reasoning
  effort *low* (as in the printed-book bake-offs), at most 6,000 output tokens.
* **Images.** The photograph is used with its background (velvet, colour bars) cut away, long side ≤ 3,000 px.
  Each provider then resizes it its own way, and the image tokens are recorded per page (billed prompt tokens
  minus those of the prompt alone).
* **Scoring.** Letters only, in one romanisation (native script in either text is transliterated). The `ortho`
  level, used in this report, folds the spelling conventions of manuscripts on both sides: anusvāra for a class
  nasal, doubled consonants after *r* (*dharmma*), *b/v*, and for Tamil/Malayalam *e/ē, o/ō*, and for Tamil voicing.
  The `strict` level is in the tables file and ranks the same.
* **Readings the manuscript really has** are counted apart from misreadings. Where at least three strong models
  (from at least two families, and only those that read the page within 10 points of the best) agree on the same
  reading against the edition, that reading goes into the reference (`CER ms`). This produced 873 variant letters
  on the Devanāgarī, Jaina and commentary pages. **I checked eight such spots on two folios by eye, and all eight are
  real scribal readings.** Examples: Sringeri 1785 *anirdeśyavapu viṣṇu vīro* without the *r* of *viṣṇur*,
  *mahātejo* for *mahātejā*; Naithani Devīmāhātmya *kīrtayiṣyanti manujā śatākṣīm iti mā tataḥ* for *manujāḥ …
  māṃ*. A dittography the scribe underlined to cancel (Sringeri f. 12, l. 7) is transcribed by every model and
  costs all of them equally.
* **Excess error** is an engine's CER minus the best engine's on the same page, as in the printed-book
  bake-offs. It removes the floor that the edition's own differences and slips set.
* **Cost** is the amount OpenRouter billed per call (`usage.cost`), with seconds measured per call.
* **Refused** means a refusal note or fewer than 30 letters. **Looped** means the same words repeated up to the
  token limit. **Cut off** means the answer stopped mid-way: Gemini's recitation filter (`finish_reason:
  content_filter`, still there after two retries) or an `error` finish with the text half written.

| provider | paper page (2:1 or 1:1.4) | wide palm leaf (6–11:1) | rule |

| Gemini (Flash, Flash Lite, Argon, Pro) | 1,080 | 1,060–1,110 | a **fixed budget per image**, whatever its size or shape |
| Claude (all) | 3,200–4,750 | **918** | ≈ pixels / 750 after scaling into its limits, so a thin leaf gets few tokens |
| GPT-6 (Sol, Astra, Luna) | 3,950–8,120 | 1,130–1,240 | 512-px tiles after scaling |

of what it gets on a paper page.

texts (Gītā, stotras), where recitation from memory is easiest.

**Damage.** Water-damaged Bengali-script Gītā with commentaries (CC0):

Opus 5 %, Argon 10 %, Flash 20 %, Pro 31 %. Sonnet 5.5 marks most of it `[…]` (89 %). The strong models mark

**Tamil palm leaves** have no puḷḷi and no *e/ē*, *o/ō* distinction. The best reader is Argon at 25 %, 1–5 % on
the cleanest Tirukkuṟaḷ leaves, against Opus 52 % and Pro 62 %. Pro also looped on three leaves. **Grantha** is
Argon's or nothing: Argon read a Whish Gītā leaf at 3 %, where every other model, Pro included, produced 80–100 %
(fluent text not on the leaf). The Kaivalyopaniṣad leaf was read best by Pro (2 %) and Flash (12 %), and the small
CSU Grantha leaves (1,577 px wide) by no one.

## 5. What makes manuscripts harder

1. **Scripts the models have rarely seen.** The models are trained on far more printed Devanāgarī than on
   anything else here. Śāradā, Nepālākṣarā/Bhujimol, Grantha and Odia, and Malayalam and Tamil in their palm-leaf
   forms, are where every model fails or invents. Within Nāgarī, the Jaina pṛṣṭhamātrā vowels and old letter
   forms cost the cheaper models 15–30 points.
2. **No word spacing and long ligatures.** A misread akṣara can't be repaired from the word boundary. A model that
   half-sees the line fills it with plausible Sanskrit of its own. This is the most dangerous failure, because it
   looks fine: Pro on Śāradā and Grantha, Flash on palm leaves.
3. **Abbreviations, corrections and dittographies.** The scribe's *jāva* formulae, the Viṣṇusahasranāma's
   underlined repeat, marginal insertions (`M:`). Models transcribe what is there, while the edition differs.
   That is a reference problem more than an OCR one; the `ms` consensus handles part of it.
4. **Layout.** Tripāṭha commentary pages, marginal commentaries on Jaina folios, glosses between the lines, two
   leaves in one photograph. The strong models keep the reading order; the weaker ones drop or mix blocks.
5. **Damage and faint ink.** Water stains, worm holes, incised leaves without ink, faded birch bark. Strong
   models mark these `[…]`; weak ones guess.
6. **Aspect ratio and image tokens.** A 6–11:1 leaf gets ~900–1,100 image tokens from Claude and Gemini, so each
   akṣara is a few pixels after the provider's resizing. Cutting the leaf into pieces gives Argon 3× the budget
   for ≈ $0.004 more a page and ≈ 10 points.
7. **The texts are famous.** Models know the Gītā, the Saddharmapuṇḍarīka and the Devīmāhātmya. That helps them
   read, but it leads to edition readings written over the manuscript (2–14 % of variant letters). It also trips
   Gemini's **recitation filter**, which stops the answer mid-way.
   It fired on 31 of ≈ 330 Gemini page readings. Two retries recovered 19 of them; 12 stayed cut, nothing billed. Another 15 Gemini
   answers ended in an "error" finish with the text half written. Both happen mostly on canonical texts
   (Aṣṭasāhasrikā, Saddharmapuṇḍarīka, Pañcarakṣā, Gītā). Expect missing lines on canonical texts read with
   Gemini; retry, cut the leaf into smaller pieces, or fall back to another model.
8. **Less text per page.** Folios carry a third as many letters as printed pages, so per-image and per-request
   costs weigh 3× more per letter.

## 6. Recommendation

| material | model | expected CER (letters, ortho) | $ / page | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Devanāgarī on paper, clean | **Gemini 3.8 Flash** for bulk; Argon on request or re-read (Opus 5.5 / Pro equal to Argon, 3× its price) | Flash 3 %, Argon 2 % | 0.003 / 0.0056 | as good as printed-book OCR; re-read with Argon any page Flash leaves cut off or short |
| Devanāgarī, hard hands, Nepalese Devanāgarī, damaged, commentary pages | **Gemini 4 Argon** | 5–17 % (fast cursive 30–50 %) | 0.007–0.013 | Opus gives up or paraphrases on the fastest cursive |
| Jaina Nāgarī | **Gemini 4 Argon** or **Opus 5.5** | 10–35 % | 0.010 / 0.032 | Flash misreads the pṛṣṭhamātrā |
| Bengali script on paper | Argon or Opus | ≈ 20–45 % (1–2 pages measured) | 0.014 / 0.029 | too few pages to rank |
| Tamil palm leaf | **Gemini 4 Argon**, leaf cut into pieces | 1–30 % (clean Kuṟaḷ to dark Tēvāram) | 0.007 | others 40–90 % |
| Malayalam, Grantha palm leaf | Argon in pieces, **with human checking** | 10–70 % | 0.008 | some leaves read, most not |
| Śāradā, Nepalese palm leaf, Odia palm leaf | **none is usable** (Argon least bad, 60–80 %) | | | needs a trained model or people |

* **Expected cost with Argon:** $0.008 a page, ≈ $0.010 per 1,000 letters of text. That is ≈ $0.60 for a Gītā
  manuscript (~75 pages), ≈ $3 for a 400-page manuscript, ≈ $20 for a Rāmāyaṇa. Batch pricing halves these.
  Readers' OCR on request already uses Argon (`contrib.OCR_ENGINE`), so a manuscript scan can go through the
  same path. For leaves more than 3.5 times as wide as high, send pieces (`prepared(…, "split")` in the script):
  ≈ $0.013 a page.
* **Don't use Flash for bulk manuscript OCR beyond clean Devanāgarī**: elsewhere it is 6–30 points behind Argon,
  to save about $0.005 a page. **Don't use Gemini 3.1 Pro for re-reads of manuscripts**: it costs 3× Argon and
  loops at low effort (14 of 67 pages). **Don't cascade.**
* **Open models** (Qwen3-VL-235B, Qwen3.8-27B, Gemma 4 31B) are not usable on manuscripts today: 42–76 % on
  clean Devanāgarī, loops on half the pages. A self-hosted model is not an option without training.
* **Fine-tuning an own model?**
  * Not worth it for **Devanāgarī**: Argon is at 1–2 % on clean hands and 5–17 % on hard ones, for under a cent a
    page.
  * Worth a **pilot** for the scripts where every model fails: Śāradā, Nepālākṣarā/Bhujimol, Grantha, Odia, and
    perhaps Malayalam palm leaves. That is if the library wants their manuscripts read rather than only
    catalogued. This test set's method gives the training data almost for free: manuscripts of works the library
    has as e-texts, aligned page by page.
  * The pilot would take ≈ 2,000–5,000 aligned lines per script (a few hundred folios), plus synthetic lines
    rendered with the Noto Śāradā / Grantha / Newa fonts. It would fine-tune Qwen3-VL (open, LoRA on one GPU for
    a day or two, ≈ $50–200 of cloud GPU per script) or a Gemini Flash (Vertex tuning). It succeeds if the model
    beats Argon's 60–80 % on these pages by a wide margin. Until then, those manuscripts should be catalogued
    (images + metadata) and transcribed by people.

## 7. Re-running when new models appear

```
python scripts/m4/bakeoff.py fetch                         # images back from their URLs (raw_data/m4/img)
python scripts/m4/bakeoff.py run --engines <new>           # add the model to ENGINES first (OpenRouter id)
python scripts/m4/bakeoff.py run --engines <new> --variant split   # wide leaves in pieces
python scripts/m4/bakeoff.py align                         # only if references changed
python scripts/m4/bakeoff.py score                         # → docs/work-2026-10h/M4-tables.md
```

All transcriptions are cached (`raw_data/m4/out/<variant>/<engine>/<page>.txt` + billed $, tokens, seconds), so a
new model costs ≈ $0.01–0.10 × 69 pages. If a new model reads a page that no engine could be located on, `align`
will locate it from that reading and the page's lower bound becomes a real score. `raw_data/m4/` is project data
(kept with the library's raw data, not in git). It holds `testset.yaml` (URLs, licences, passages), `ref/`, `gt/`,
the agents' source notes (`candidates/`), every reading (`out/`), and the scores (`scores*.json`, `variants.json`).

## 8. Limits

* 69 pages: per-script numbers rest on 3–10 pages each, and Bengali on two. Treat differences under ~5 points
  within a script as noise.
* Pages located by a single reading (several palm leaves) take that reading's span, which favours it slightly.
  Pages that no engine could be located on give only lower bounds.
* Reasoning effort was low except in the one experiment above. Prompts were not tuned per script. Tamil and
  Malayalam were asked for in ISO 15919: a native-script prompt might do better for the Dravidian leaves.
* References are editions, not transcriptions of these manuscripts. The `ms` consensus and the excess metric
  correct for that, but not fully (Jaina, the garbled Raghuvaṃśa page).
