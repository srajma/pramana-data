## 4. Fixes R4 made on the way

* **Unread editions** (`scripts/r3/new_finds.py`): a book counted as read once any page was held, so a reader stopped
  after its probe pages left the rest unread for good — 1,378 of R3's and R4's accepted editions. It now counts the
  pages still to read against the book's cached page list (read when all but 3 % are held).
* **Memory, on 4 GB:** `new_finds.py` reads `catalog/scans.yaml` line by line (a YAML parse of its 17 MB took
  ≈ 950 MB) and hands the reader only its entries (`scans ocr --entries`); `scripts/r3/spend.py` streams the 300 MB
  ledger (the watchdog read it whole every 2 min); libyaml for `scans.yaml` elsewhere; the hunt runs in fresh chunks
  (`scripts/r4/hunt_loop.sh`; it grows ≈ 100 MB an hour); the reading process is the OOM victim (`oom_score_adj`) and
  resumes; staging forks share the catalogue (`gc.freeze`), though one worker is still the only way that fits.
* **Garbled page stores:** readers killed mid-write left 5 book files with zero-filled or bad gzip members, which
  stopped staging. `scanstore` now keeps what it read before the damage; `scripts/r4/repair_gz.py` recovers every
  intact member (the 5 files kept 263, 23, 96, 850 and 671 pages; the lost pages are read again by the next reader).
* **Counting:** a redlink accepted because a book found for another prints it (`accept (shared)`) is not a search;
  `remainder.py`, `scripts/r4/status.py` and `scripts/r4/rates.py` count searches by their queries.
* **Watchdog** (`scripts/r3/cap.sh CAP RESERVE SINCE`): a follow-up counts its own ledger from `SINCE` (this branch's
  refworks ledger lacks R3's NCC and classifier rows, so "R3's total" here was $1,721, not $1,918); it keeps watching
  while the reader loop or the hunt loop lives.

## 6. What would make the rest cheaper

1. **A direct Gemini API key** (AI Studio batch, inline images or the File API): half price on reading, which was 90 %
   of R4's spend. OpenRouter's batches take only public image URLs, and Google's fetcher obeys Cloudflare tunnels'
   robots.txt (R3).
2. **More of the books to Haiku** (or a cheaper reader for DLI scans): Haiku read 201,021 pages at $1.63 per 1,000,
   Flash 322,712 at $3.60. Only books whose two probe pages agree with Flash go to Haiku (half of them); DLI bitonal
   scans and lithographs go to Flash. A bake-off of cheaper vision models on DLI scans (Gemini Flash-Lite,
   open-weight models on a rented GPU) could halve the Flash share.
3. **The shared pass over everything already read.** 605 redlinks were filled from books found for others, at no
   reading cost. The hunt checks only the books of its own run against the redlinks; one pass of all 7,307 staged
   books' contents against the ≈ 98,000 remaining redlinks (verify calls only, ≈ $0.002 each) would find more of these
   free fills before any further search.
4. **Skip the branches that do not pay:** Dharma (3.4 %), Kāvya (4.3 %), Āyurveda (0 of 34), Bauddha and Jaina
   (5–6 %) in the random sample. The ranking already puts them last; a follow-up can stop at ≈ 5 %.
5. **Other edition sources:** the Indian Culture portal's 60,636 rare-book PDFs and HathiTrust full-view volumes (the
   hunt uses HathiTrust only for hints) hold editions archive.org does not; they need adapters, not more money.
6. **A bigger machine** (8 GB) saves time, not money: the hunt (≈ 1.9 GB) and a 64-worker reader (≈ 1.5 GB) could run
   together, and staging could fork. On 4 GB R4 alternated: ≈ 2.6 days of hunting and reading, then ≈ 9 h of staging.

## Lessons (also in the wiki, memories/comprehensiveness)

* Estimate what is left from a random sample of it, not from the outcomes of an earlier selection: R3's searches were
  chosen by priority, and the rates they gave were twice what remained.
* "Read" must mean all pages held, not any: a probe page is a page.
* On a 4 GB machine every whole-file parse matters (a 17 MB YAML is ≈ 950 MB in Python; a 300 MB ledger read whole
  every 2 minutes); the harness's memory guard kills the process with the highest `oom_score`, so mark the
  expendable one.
* A process killed while appending to a gzip file can leave zeros in it; readers must tolerate a bad member.
* Staging is not free: locating works inside multi-work books and front checks cost ≈ 3 % of the reading.
